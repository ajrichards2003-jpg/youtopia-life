Can a gut-made compound help a stiff heart relax?

Urolithin A improved measures of heart relaxation in mice and lab-grown human tissue. Here’s what that tells us about the heart, and where the viral 80% figure came from.

Heart failure evidence · Experimental

Youtopia Life editorial · 26 November 2026

A heartbeat has two jobs. First the heart squeezes. Then it relaxes so it can fill again. In heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, or HFpEF, the heart can still pump out a normal proportion of the blood it holds, while impaired relaxation or stiffness makes it harder to fill. That distinction is what makes a study about the moment between beats worth reading.

What changed in the lab?

In an August 2026 Science Advances paper, researchers gave urolithin A directly to mice with an experimental form of HFpEF. After a week, selected ultrasound measures of relaxation and filling improved, and the researchers saw less scarring and enlargement of heart muscle cells. Key comparisons involved five to seven mice per group. Su et al., 2026

The team identified a specific site on a protein called PKGIα involved in muscle relaxation. When the researchers altered that site in mice, most of the protective effects disappeared. Urolithin A also changed the speed of relaxation and contraction in lab-grown human heart tissue. A separate team saw related results in another mouse model and implicated mitophagy, the process by which cells clear damaged mitochondria. Su et al., 2026 · Separate mouse study, 2026

Where the 80% figure came from

King’s College London reported that some measures improved by up to 80% versus untreated mice. Its release does not identify the exact measure or calculation behind that figure. It gives readers no estimate of how much a person’s heart function would improve. University announcement

What have human trials found?

We found no published trial in people with HFpEF. A four-week crossover trial in 10 people with reduced ejection fraction, a different form of heart failure, found no significant benefit on its heart ultrasound or blood measurements. A separate October 2026 trial in 50 adults with obesity found no significant improvement in its primary measure of blood-vessel function. Heart failure trial · Blood-vessel function trial

Small human trials have reported gains in selected muscle strength or endurance tests, while several primary outcomes, including peak cycling power and six-minute walking distance, missed statistical significance. A four-week trial also found changes in some T-cell measures. It did not assess infections or vaccine responses. The maker of Mitopure supported the immune trial and the two foundational muscle trials. Muscle evidence review · Immune-cell trial

Where do pomegranates fit?

Some gut microbes convert compounds found in pomegranates, walnuts and certain berries into urolithin A. In a 100-person pomegranate-juice study, about 40% showed substantial conversion after the challenge. Taking urolithin A directly produced more consistent blood levels. Researchers have yet to establish a blood level needed for a health benefit. Human bioavailability study

Youtopia evidence rating · Experimental

The heart findings justify a trial in people with HFpEF. Outcomes that matter to patients include breathlessness, exercise capacity, and quality of life. This research gives people no basis to change their prescribed heart-failure care. Australian heart failure care guidance
