# Bovine colostrum: what does the evidence really show?

**Status: editorial draft — not approved for publication**  
**Evidence label: Promising / limited, depending on outcome**  
**Last reviewed: 1 November 2026**

Bovine colostrum is the first milk produced by cows after calving. It contains immunoglobulins, lactoferrin, proteins and other bioactive compounds. It is not equivalent to human colostrum, and its composition differs from ordinary milk.

## What researchers are investigating

- **Gut barrier:** Some small human studies have explored intestinal permeability, particularly under exercise stress. This does not establish treatment for 'leaky gut' or gastrointestinal disease.
- **Infections and immunity:** Trials have investigated respiratory and gastrointestinal outcomes, but populations, formulations and study quality vary. Avoid blanket claims that it 'boosts immunity'.
- **Athletic recovery:** Performance and recovery findings are mixed and cannot be generalized to all users.
- **Children:** Evidence is too limited and heterogeneous to recommend routine use for healthy children. Pediatric findings require their own appraisal, not extrapolation from adults.

## Safety and practical questions

Bovine colostrum is derived from cow's milk and may be unsuitable for people with cow's milk protein allergy. Parents should discuss supplements with a qualified pediatric clinician, particularly where a child has allergies, chronic illness or takes medication. Product quality, microbial controls, processing, allergen statements and dose studied in clinical trials all matter.
