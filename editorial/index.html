Our editorial promise

We explain mechanisms, examine evidence, report uncertainty and help readers find original sources. We also feature products and may earn affiliate revenue, with disclosures beside relevant links. Commercial interest does not determine our evidence assessment.

How we report a health claim

  1. Identify the claim: who said it, which preparation or intervention, for whom and for what outcome?
  2. Trace the source: interviews are discovery leads; original studies and authoritative guidance support the reporting.
  3. Label the evidence: established clinical use, human randomised trial, observational association, animal experiment, cell experiment or unverified claim.
  4. Explain scale and limits: sample size, duration, comparison group, meaningful outcomes, null results and conflicts.
  5. Separate the mechanism from the result: plausible biology does not prove a treatment works; a biomarker change does not prove longer life.
  6. Give safety context: label directions, interactions and relevant clinician checks, without personalised diagnosis or prescribing.

Education and promotion carry different responsibilities

An “educational purposes” disclaimer does not make every claim appropriate or turn promotional content into independent journalism. Australian TGA guidance prohibits advertising prescription medicines to the public and explains that context, links and implied encouragement can matter. We report medicine research neutrally without purchase links, dosing regimens, endorsement language or calls to obtain a prescription for a speculative benefit.

For supplements, we distinguish the studied ingredient from a retail brand and check the particular product’s permitted claims and label before a promotion. Required advertising statements depend on the product and presentation; a generic disclaimer is not a substitute.

People, expertise and commercial relationships

We identify speakers’ relevant expertise and commercial interests when reporting their claims. A company founder is not automatically a medical specialist; a prominent researcher is not the sole authority on a field. Featuring a person, institution or product does not imply their endorsement of Youtopia or blanket endorsement by us.

Sponsored, affiliate and editorial content should be clearly identified at the point readers encounter it. We disclose AI-assisted research and writing. We do not describe an article as medically reviewed unless an appropriately qualified reviewer has actually reviewed it.

Sources, dates and corrections

New research explainers show the research-check date and direct sources. We distinguish peer-reviewed studies from preprints and discuss retractions or substantial corrections when relevant. New findings may change the assessment; a review date is not a promise of continuous monitoring.

Report an error through Contact Youtopia, including the article, disputed statement and supporting source. We assess it, correct material errors and record substantive updates in the article. We protect patient privacy, respect copyright and avoid fabricated personal testimonials.

A resource for different ages

Adult studies do not establish suitability for children. Content stays suitable for a broad audience; medicine and supplement decisions for children require appropriate professional advice. Body Explorer remains a work in progress while its educational accuracy and usability are developed.

Before making a health decision

For educational purposes only; not personal medical advice, diagnosis or a treatment plan. Always read the label and follow directions for use. Check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting supplements or changing medicines, particularly during pregnancy, for children, or if you have a medical condition. Do not stop prescribed treatment because of an article.
