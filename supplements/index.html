Supplements,
without the guesswork.

When to take them, what to take them with, which combinations have evidence and where the risks sit. Start with the purpose and your own context, then read the label.

Before building a routine

Make a list of everything you take, including prescribed medicines and overlapping ingredients. Check whether a deficiency or specific goal is established, and add one change at a time. Timing rarely rescues a supplement that is unnecessary or interacts with medication. This guide is general education; a pharmacist or clinician can check your exact products and doses.

Better combinations

Three pairings with a reason behind them.

01 / Training

Creatine + progressive resistance training

The strongest “stack” here pairs a studied supplement with the stimulus it helps support. Keep training, protein intake and recovery central.

02 / Plant iron

Beans or lentils + vitamin-C-rich food

Add capsicum, citrus or tomato to a legume meal. Vitamin C improves absorption of non-heme iron from plant food; this is a food pairing, not an instruction to take iron pills.

03 / Bone-health context

Vitamin D + enough dietary calcium, when needed

Consider baseline intake and vitamin D status. Correction of a shortfall can matter; automatically adding high doses of both is not a universal longevity strategy.

Four label checks that save confusion

  1. Count the active amount: elemental magnesium and zinc, EPA/DHA within fish oil, and the full vitamin D total across products.
  2. Watch overlapping ingredients: two “sleep” or “longevity” blends can duplicate minerals, herbs or stimulants.
  3. Separate evidence for the ingredient from evidence for the brand: delivery, dose and human outcomes are different questions.
  4. Review medication and procedure plans: bleeding-risk supplements, vitamin K and blood-glucose-active products deserve a pharmacist or clinician check.
